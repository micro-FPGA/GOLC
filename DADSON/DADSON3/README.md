**DADSON-3: The Breadboard Morning**

It was a Saturday morning when the father placed the small board on the kitchen table.

18mm by 55mm. Smaller than a chocolate bar. It fit directly into the breadboard without adapters, without custom carrier boards, without soldering. Just push it in and start.

"What is it?" his son asked.

"It's a computer," the father said. "A real one."

The boy was skeptical. He had seen computers. They were large things with fans.

"This one has 40 pins," the father continued. "Like the old chips from before you were born. But inside it has a Lattice Certus-NX FPGA — 40,000 logic cells. And 32 megabytes of HyperRAM. Enough to run Linux."

The boy looked at it. Then at the breadboard. Then back.

"Can we connect something to it?"

That was the right question. That was always the right question.

They connected an LED to one of the 34 I/O pins. Then a button. Then a second LED. The USB-C cable went to the laptop. The JTAG connection appeared automatically.

By lunchtime the LEDs were blinking in a pattern the boy had programmed himself — his first FPGA logic, written in the kitchen on a Saturday morning, running on a board smaller than his hand.

The father said nothing. He just watched.

Some things don't need explaining. A blinking LED at the right moment says everything.

And the DIPSY-40NX sat in its breadboard, 40 pins in a row, waiting for the next question.
