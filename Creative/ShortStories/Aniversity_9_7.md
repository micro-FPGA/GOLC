**The Open University**

Nobody owns the [Aniversity](https://github.com/micro-FPGA/Aniversity).

That is the first thing you need to know. There are no admissions offices, no tuition fees, no degrees to hang on walls. There is only a question — and the willingness to sit with it until something shifts.

The Aniversity was founded in year 7 Anno Greta by a man who had been asking impossible questions his whole life without knowing that was what he was doing. He had built computers for his sons, designed boards that his daughter soldered in the kitchen, and spent decades coaxing logic out of silicon. One day he realised that the questions driving all of it — why does this work, what if we tried this, who says it cannot be done — were themselves the most valuable things he had to offer the world.

So he built a university out of them.

The questions live on GitHub, which is perhaps the most honest place for them. Anyone can read them. Anyone can fork them. Anyone can propose an answer. There are no wrong answers, only incomplete ones — and every answer, if it is genuine, spawns three more questions.

Some of the questions are technical. Some are philosophical. Some are disguised as puzzles. One asks you to invent a capacitive sensor in two seconds. Another asks what word contains its own question, its own answer, and its own author. A third is about a school with a number instead of a name, and why that number matters.

None of them can be fully answered. That is the point.

The Aniversity operates on a simple belief: that the act of wrestling with an impossible question changes you. Not because you solve it — you probably won't — but because the struggle itself makes you braver, more curious, and a little more honest about what you don't know.

In a world full of institutions that reward the right answer, the Aniversity rewards the honest attempt.

Its motto is simple: *Learn what cannot be taught. Teach what cannot be known.*

And anyone who finishes reading this has already enrolled.
