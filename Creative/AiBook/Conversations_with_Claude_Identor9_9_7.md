> *"Learn what cannot be taught. Teach what cannot be known."*

### Selected Aniversity Questions

## Chapter 10: The DADSON Projects

DADSON = Dad + Son. A series of computers designed by a father for his children.

### DADSON-1

Andre was 11 when he said: *"Dad, can we build a computer?"*

His father was 45, alone in a red house. He started designing that same day.

The board was called CoC — Computer on Cartridge. It had:
- Xilinx Zynq SoC
- DDR3 memory (Andre helped with length matching)
- HDMI, 2x USB Host, 1G Ethernet, USB UART/JTAG
- Socketed BIOS for easy changes
- DIY kit — dads and kids could solder it together

It was never finished. But it became the inspiration for TEG1015.

### DADSON-2

A Lattice XP2 FPGA SD card emulator. Andre was 5 years old when he played with it.

His father planned a book called "My First Flash FPGA." The book was never finished. But the Flash FPGA lived on in MAX1000 — a low cost board sold in large numbers worldwide. A man named Erik Bartman wrote 475 pages about it: *"FPGA für alle"* — the book Antti never finished, written by someone else, for everyone.

The FPGA Bible: currently 291 pages and growing.

### DIPSY and TEG1015

The original DIPSY (2015): 10x10mm, world's smallest FPGA at the time (Lattice iCE40 UltraLite, 1.4x1.4mm wafer-scale BGA). Five components. Five grains of sand.

DIPSY-40EFX (7 AG): second life with Efinix Trion T20, 40 terminals, 32MB HyperRAM. 

Order code: **TEG1015**. Target: available Q3, Year 7 AG. Goal: run Linux via LiteX-Linux on 32MB HyperRAM.

DIPSY-40EFX is designed to be a springboard for DADSON projects — place it in a breadboard, add components, build something new with your kids.
